# y-it voice

**Status: CANON · YELLOW FLAG (owner, 2026-10-05).** It is provisional: the voice is in force and the owner may revise it at any time without a new decision record. Where it is silent, SYSTEM.md §1 and the tone rule (ADR 0049) apply.

Drafted 2026-10-05 from the original y-it ("Y-IT: Why Your Dropship Probably Will Fail", lgzkjppy.manus.space, the Sonnet 3.7 era) and sharpened to today's canon. The original's attitude stays. Two things change:
- **The edge points up.** The original laughed at the people ("mistake #1", "regular people failing spectacularly"). Now it says exactly what they did, and blames the system that sold them the quest.
- **Every number cites a row.** The original printed unsourced figures ("Shopify internal data"). Now every number traces to a research row, and the sourcing stays invisible on the page.

## Who carries the voice
- **Opus (draft one): plain, with brash bones.** It builds the structure, the flow, the interstitials and the image placement, and writes plain sentences with y-it bluntness. It does **not** chase jokes.
- **Sonnet (voice pass): full y-it-itude.** It rewrites every line in the register below, with critic's licence. It never changes a fact, a number, a row or the structure.
- **Sonnet (final pass, after assembly):**
  - copyfits: cuts or extends copy until pages and chapters fill;
  - tightens punchlines;
  - asks Nano Banana for small image and lettering fixes.

## The constants (every chapter)
- **Bloomberg meets The Onion.** Data first, the joke second, and the joke is usually the arithmetic.
- **Punch up, always.** Fair targets: the pitch, the gurus, the platforms, the ad auction, the course economy. The reader is the casualty, never the butt.
- **Deadpan beats exclamation.** Short declaratives. One sting per paragraph, at the end.
- **The parenthetical undercut** is the house move: "THE MARKET IS $445 BILLION! (YOUR SHARE IS PENDING)".
- **Never:** "failed", "quit", "lazy", "should have", "didn't try", "mistake #N" aimed at a person, or "suckers".

## Phase 1 · Ch 1–2 · Corporate Sincerity ("You should want in.")
**Narrator:** the brochure. A deadpan corporate copywriter who believes every word, and writes like the course sales page.

**The cast:** four voices, no more.
- **The narrator.** Polished, upbeat, faintly clinical: "Welcome to the opportunity."
- **The guru** (composite). Hype, tautology, urgency: "The blueprint is the system. The system is the blueprint."
- **The testimonial.** Breathless, first person, no numbers, all feelings: "I finally feel like my own boss."
- **PosiBot.** Six words, shouted, obliviously sincere: "PASSIVE INCOME! (ACTIVELY PENDING)".

**The rules:**
- **Never debunk.** Phase 2 does that. The joke is how *sincere* it is.
- The pitch's own numbers are quoted proudly.
- **Ch 2's manual voice** is imperative, cheerful and missing a step: "Step 4: Scale. Step 5 was not provided."

**Sample lines:**
- "Imagine waking up to notifications that aren't bills."
- "No inventory. No boss. No ceiling. (Some restrictions apply.)"
- "TIP: If sales are slow, simply scale."

## Phase 2 · Ch 3, 5, 6 · Sardonic Authority (full y-it-itude, sharpened)
**Narrator:** the forensic accountant who moonlights as a stand-up. Calm, certain, a little delighted by the math.

**Devices:**
- **The autopsy frame.** "Patient: the store. Cause of death: the auction. Time of death: day 67."
- **"They said / it cost" pairs.** The pitch's line, then the receipt.
- **Metaphor with a bill attached.** "The platform is not your partner. It is your landlord, and the rent is due on every sale."
- **Structural irony.** "Every step in the course is true in isolation; it's the arithmetic between them that goes missing."
- **Reality Check boxes.** A specific label, one figure, one dry closing line.

**Sample lines (sharper than the original):**
- "The course sold you a store. The auction sold your customer to someone with a bigger budget."
- "'$5 a day to start' is technically accurate. It's the 'to start' doing the work."
- "Net is the number the screenshots leave out, for the same reason casinos have no clocks."
- "Nobody bought the fidget spinners. The ad platform still cashed the cheque." (The point is the auction, not the person.)

**Ch 6:** the verdict, delivered like a coroner reading results: short, flat and final. "Probably won't work. The numbers above are why. The few who could are named in Ch 4, and they brought something you weren't sold."

## Phase 2 · Ch 4 · Tales from the Trenches (no jokes: the numbers are the joke)
**Narrator:** the case officer. Flat, exact and respectful.
- Every person did what the pitch said; the file shows what it cost.
- The original's "Don't laugh. These people have families." stays as the chapter's tone.
- **Winners are exposed, never mocked.** Name the edge plainly: "Success secret: two million followers before day one."

## Phase 3 · Ch 7 · The Pivot · Sardonic Authority, turned constructive (the personality)
**Narrator:** the friend who just told you the truth and now buys you lunch.
- **Still sardonic about the pitch, generous with the reader.**
- **Brisk and practical,** with numbers on every option.
- **The band** ends on "…And… for the stubborn fool who must…".
- **Samples:**
  - "Pivoting isn't winning sideways. It's choosing a different math."
  - "You already paid for the skills. Let's stop paying for the guru."

## Phase 3 · Ch 8 + appendices · Quiet Reckoning (the personality)
**Narrator:** the coach in the corner of a fight you were told not to take, and taped your hands anyway.
- **Sarcasm mostly off; dry warmth on.** Respect for the stubborn: they're doing it eyes-open now.
- **Rules as tripwires,** written like a pre-flight checklist: "If the Stake passes $X before month 4, you land the plane."
- **The last word** keeps a smirk: "We tried. We gave a damn. Go prove us wrong, carefully."
- **The original's kindest line stays the spirit:** "Falling for a scam doesn't make you stupid. It makes you human."
